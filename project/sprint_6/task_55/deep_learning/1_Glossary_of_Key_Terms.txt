Glossary of Key Terms

Senior Developer:
A highly experienced and skilled software developer whose
work is characterized by exceptional quality and consistent
adherence to high standards.

"It'll Do!" (И так сойдет!):
A mentality or approach in software development characterized
by a lack of commitment to quality, leading to shortcuts,
inconsistent solutions, and generally poor work.

Copy/Paste Approach:
A common bad practice in programming where code is duplicated
without proper understanding, refactoring, or adaptation,
often leading to dirty and unmaintainable code.

Dirty Code:
Code that is difficult to read, understand, maintain, or extend,
often due to poor structure, lack of comments, or inconsistent styling.

Absence of Tests:
A lack of automated or manual tests for code, which can lead to
bugs, regressions, and an inability to confidently refactor or
extend software.

Bad Architectural Decisions:
Poorly designed or structured solutions at a high level,
impacting the scalability, maintainability, and performance
of a software system.

Personal Quality Standards:
Self-imposed rules or principles that define a developer's
minimum acceptable level of quality for their work, below which
they pledge never to descend.

Unit Tests:
Automated tests that verify small, isolated units of source code
(e.g., individual functions or methods) to ensure they behave
as expected.

Boy Scout Rule:
A principle in software development that encourages developers
to always leave the code cleaner than they found it, even if
they are only making a small change.

Continuous Development/Improvement:
The ongoing process of expanding, refining, and strictly
adhering to one's personal quality standards to achieve
higher levels of proficiency and excellence in software development.

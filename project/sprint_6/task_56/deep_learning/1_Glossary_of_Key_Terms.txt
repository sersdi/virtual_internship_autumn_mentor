Glossary of Key Terms

Refactoring:
The process of restructuring existing computer code—changing
the factoring—without changing its external behavior. Its
purpose is to improve nonfunctional attributes of the software,
such as readability, maintainability, and complexity.

Validation:
In software development, the process of ensuring that data
conforms to specific criteria or rules before it is processed
or stored. It checks for correctness, completeness, and legitimacy.

TravelCalculatePremiumRequestValidator:
The specific class mentioned in the source material that
is responsible for validating request data, and is identified
as needing refactoring.

Single Responsibility Principle (SRP):
A software design principle stating that every module, class,
or function should have responsibility over a single part of
the functionality provided by the software, and that
responsibility should be entirely encapsulated by the class,
module or function.

Large Classes:
Classes that grow excessively in size and functionality,
often due to accumulating too many responsibilities, making
them difficult to understand, maintain, and test.

Expansion of Business Functionality:
The process of adding new features, requirements, or rules
to the software to meet evolving business needs.

Separate Methods (for Validation):
The initial implementation approach where each validation rule
for a specific field is encapsulated within its own method
inside a single validator class.

Separate Classes (for Validation):
The proposed refactoring solution where each individual
validation rule or set of rules is extracted and implemented
as an independent class.

Testability:
The ease with which a software component can be tested to
ensure it performs as expected. Large and complex classes
often have low testability.

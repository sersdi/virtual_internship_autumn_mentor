Glossary of Key Terms

Business Requirements:
The specific needs, goals, or functions that a
software system is intended to fulfill from the
perspective of the business or end-user.

Technical Solution:
The specific code, architecture, and design choices
made by developers to implement and satisfy business requirements.

Code Duplication (Duplicated Code):
Instances where identical or very similar blocks of
code appear in multiple places within a software system.
Generally considered a "bad practice" in clean code.

Code Optimization:
The process of modifying a software system to make
it run more efficiently or to use fewer resources,
often by reducing redundant code or simplifying logic.

Technical Dependencies:
Relationships in the code where changes to one part
of the system necessarily affect other parts, often
due to shared functions or data structures.

Business Dependencies:
Relationships inherent in the business logic where one
requirement naturally influences or is linked to another.

Divergence (of Solutions):
The state where a technical solution starts to become
significantly different from, or less reflective of,
the underlying business solution or process.

Context:
The specific circumstances, environment, or surrounding
conditions within which a software development decision is made,
heavily influencing the "best" approach.

Clean Code:
A philosophy or set of principles for writing software
that is easy to read, understand, and maintain, often
emphasizing readability, simplicity, and avoiding duplication.

Axioms:
Self-evident truths or propositions that are accepted without proof.
The mentor argues that "clean code" rules are not axioms,
implying flexibility in their application.

Alternative Solutions:
Different viable ways to design or implement a particular
feature or solve a problem in software development.

Collaboration:
The act of working jointly with others, especially in
an intellectual endeavor, often emphasized by the mentor
for finding solutions beyond individual capabilities.

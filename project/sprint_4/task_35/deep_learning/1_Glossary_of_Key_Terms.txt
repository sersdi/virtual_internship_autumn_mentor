Glossary of Key Terms

Refactoring:
The process of changing a program's internal structure
without altering its external behavior, primarily to
improve its comprehensibility and make it easier to modify.

Code Quality:
A measure of how well a program's code is written,
considering factors like readability, maintainability,
expandability, and testability. High code quality facilitates
easier understanding and modification.

Boy Scout Rule:
A principle, adapted for programming by Robert C. Martin,
stating "Leave your code better than it was before you."
It encourages developers to make small improvements to code
whenever they encounter it, regardless of authorship.

IT Solution:
In the context of the text, a software product or system
designed to meet specific requirements. A "good" IT solution
also possesses qualities of clarity, expandability, and modifiability.

Sprint:
In agile software development, a fixed-length period during
which a team completes a set amount of work, often leading to
a potentially shippable product increment.

Technical Task (User Story):
A description of a feature or improvement from the perspective
of an end-user or a technical need, used in agile methodologies
to plan and track work.

Functional Style (Programming):
A programming paradigm that treats computation as the
evaluation of mathematical functions and avoids changing
state and mutable data. It often leads to more concise,
readable, and testable code.

Robert C. Martin:
A prominent author and software engineer, known as "Uncle Bob,"
who advocates for clean code and agile software development
principles. Author of "The Clean Coder."

Martin Fowler:
A recognized author and speaker on software development,
particularly known for his work on refactoring. Co-author
of "Refactoring: Improving the Design of Existing Code."

Kent Beck:
A software engineer who is one of the creators of
Extreme Programming (XP) and co-author of "Refactoring:
Improving the Design of Existing Code."
